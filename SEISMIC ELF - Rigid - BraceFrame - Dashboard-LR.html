Frame stiffness ·

Relative lateral stiffness of each braced frame, story by story, for the rigid-diaphragm distribution (section 9). One pinned diagonal per story; its axial stiffness projected to the horizontal:

Brace length Lbr = √(Lb² + h²),   cos θ = Lb / Lbr  (θ = brace angle from horizontal)
Lateral stiffness k = (A·E / Lbr)·cos²θ = A·E·Lb² / Lbr³  (one cos θ for the force, one for the displacement)
Two-bay X (2X): both braces in a story see the same drift (beams and columns axially rigid), so they act in parallel: k = k₁ + k₂, each with its own bay length (L₁ or L₂) and the story height. The story shear splits between them k₁ : k₂.
E = 29,000 ksi is the same for every brace, so only the ratios matter. k is shown in kip/in for reference. h is each story's own height (section 3), so the share of each frame can change from story to story.
Center of rigidity, this story

Assumptions

  1. Single frames: one diagonal brace per story. Two-bay X frames (2X): one brace per bay per story, meeting at the middle column (Λ in one story, V in the next; together an X over two stories). All braces pinned at both ends; beams and columns pinned (no frame action).
  2. Work points at the column centerlines and floor lines. No gussets and no rigid offsets: Lbr is the calculated work-point length.
  3. The brace is elastic in tension and compression, with the same E for every brace.
  4. Columns and beams are axially rigid (column axial deformation is ignored for now).
  5. One brace area A per frame, used at every story. Each story uses its own height h from section 3.
  6. The diaphragm force at each level is distributed with the stiffness of the story directly below that level (sections 9 to 11). Story shears in section 12 are distributed with the stiffness of that story.

12Braced Frame Forces Story shear, brace force, column axial Envelope of ±5% accidental torsion

About this method

Story shears use Fx (section 6). The story shear Vi = ΣFx at and above level i is distributed to the frames with the frame stiffness of story i (rigid model: translation and torsion about that story's center of rigidity; flexible models: tributary line reactions, split among frames on a line by stiffness). The force the frame takes at each level is the difference of its story shears below and above. With "Envelope of ±5%", the +5% and −5% cases are both solved and the one giving the larger base overturning moment is shown.

Each frame is a pinned truss with work points at the column centerlines. Single frames have one diagonal per story; two-bay X frames have one per bay, meeting at the middle column, and the story shear splits between them by stiffness (k₁ : k₂). Brace force P = Vbrace / cos θ, tension when the brace rises toward the end the load pushes toward. Column forces come from joint equilibrium, working down from the roof: at each joint the column below carries the column above plus the vertical components of the braces that meet there. For a single frame this is the same as the overturning moment at the floor below (or above) divided by Lb. The start-end column is in tension for force in +X / +Y. Every story is checked for ΣFx = ΣFy = ΣM = 0. These are ELF forces (Eh); gravity loads and AISC 341 capacity-design forces are not included.

9Horizontal Distribution Rigid diaphragm

About this method

The typical floor plan is collapsed into a beam spanning perpendicular to the force. LFRS lines parallel to the force are the supports. The load per foot is w = (F / A) × d, where d is the plan depth at that location, so the load steps up or down wherever the plan changes depth. With Seismic weight from load areas on (section 3), the load follows the weight instead: w = F × m / W, where m is the weight per foot of the cut (psf × length of each load area it crosses) and W is the floor weight, and the center of mass is the weight centroid.

10Collector Diagrams Design Fpx only Envelope of ±5% accidental torsion

About this method

Along the support line, the diaphragm delivers unit shear vu = V / d from each side where the diaphragm exists (left and right sides add where both are present). Each braced frame takes its share R as a point load where the brace from the story below meets this level (the top end of that brace, set by the brace direction in section 2). The collector force is C(t) = ∫vu dt, integrated from the start of the diaphragm along the line, with a drop of R at each brace connection. The value at the far end is not forced: it closes to zero only because ∫vu = ΣR, and it is reported below as the backcheck.

At a floor where the brace from the story above starts at the other end of the bay (stacked braces), the frame beam also carries that brace's story shear between the two columns. That is a frame force, not a collector force; it is listed separately below. Where the two braces meet at the same column (alternating braces), there is no through-force.

Rigid diaphragm, torsion only: lines perpendicular to the load take only torsional forces. For those lines the diaphragm shear comes from the torsional forces alone, so vu between lines equals the running sum of the torsional forces, and each collector still closes.

Envelope of ±5% (rigid model, "Envelope of ±5%" selected above): the collector is solved for both the +5% and the −5% case. The diagram shows both, and the design value at each point is the larger magnitude of the two. Between break points C is linear, so the envelope peaks at a break point. Ω0, when applied, multiplies the design Fpx collector force at every break point (both sides of each brace connection), whichever Fpx limit governs.

ChordsModule 11

11Diaphragm Chord Forces T = C = M / d

About this method

Builds the same beam as section 9 (same loading, supports and diagrams), with its own level, direction, force and model choices above, independent of section 9. At the location chosen below, M is taken from the beam, and the plan is cut perpendicular to the beam at that location. This is the same cut used to build the load w = (F / A)·d. The chords are the outer edges of the diaphragm along that cut, and d is the distance between them. At a step in the plan the smaller depth is used. Chord force T = C = M / d, with M taken about the mid-depth between the chords. With the rigid model, perpendicular frames push along the beam axis; their running sum is an axial force N across the cut. N is reported separately and is not added to T = C. With positive M and force in +X or +Y, the chord on the side the load pushes toward is in tension. Seismic load reverses, so design both chords for ±T.

    Frame stiffness ·

    Relative lateral stiffness of each braced frame, story by story, for the rigid-diaphragm distribution (section 9). One pinned diagonal per story; its axial stiffness projected to the horizontal:

    Brace length Lbr = √(Lb² + h²),   cos θ = Lb / Lbr  (θ = brace angle from horizontal)
    Lateral stiffness k = (A·E / Lbr)·cos²θ = A·E·Lb² / Lbr³  (one cos θ for the force, one for the displacement)
    Two-bay X (2X): both braces in a story see the same drift (beams and columns axially rigid), so they act in parallel: k = k₁ + k₂, each with its own bay length (L₁ or L₂) and the story height. The story shear splits between them k₁ : k₂.
    E = 29,000 ksi is the same for every brace, so only the ratios matter. k is shown in kip/in for reference. h is each story's own height (section 3), so the share of each frame can change from story to story.
    Center of rigidity, this story

    Assumptions

    1. Single frames: one diagonal brace per story. Two-bay X frames (2X): one brace per bay per story, meeting at the middle column (Λ in one story, V in the next; together an X over two stories). All braces pinned at both ends; beams and columns pinned (no frame action).
    2. Work points at the column centerlines and floor lines. No gussets and no rigid offsets: Lbr is the calculated work-point length.
    3. The brace is elastic in tension and compression, with the same E for every brace.
    4. Columns and beams are axially rigid (column axial deformation is ignored for now).
    5. One brace area A per frame, used at every story. Each story uses its own height h from section 3.
    6. The diaphragm force at each level is distributed with the stiffness of the story directly below that level (sections 9 to 11). Story shears in section 12 are distributed with the stiffness of that story.
